# Telling a Yuma video

This guidance is distilled from previous promo videos and the review rounds they went through. Treat it as the current house style, and update it when brand owners refine it.

## Structure

A Yuma product video moves from a claim, to proof, to an invitation:

1. **Title** (≈5 s) — the product name and one line that says what it is.
2. **Statement** (≈5 s) — the idea being left behind, struck through, replaced by the idea being proposed. *One agent answers.* → *A team delivers.*
3. **Show it working** (the longest part) — the product doing its thing, one capability per beat, each beat with one caption.
4. **Under the hood** (short) — how it is made: configuration, components, tools, deployment. Fast, list-like scenes.
5. **The real thing** — real screenshots of the product, so the viewer knows it exists.
6. **Closing statement** — the same struck-through device as the opening, one level up: what category this is *not*, and what it *is*.
7. **Outro** — on the Forest Green Yuma background: product name, the one action to take, "Powered by Yuma".

Open and close with the same rhetorical device; the symmetry makes the closing land.

### Scaling it down

The seven parts are for a product video of one to two minutes. For a short piece — an announcement, an event, a teaser of 15 to 30 seconds — keep four: **title, statement, one "show it" beat, outro**. Drop "under the hood", the screenshots and the closing statement. One idea, three or four scenes, four or five narration lines.

When the subject is not a product, the same rules hold with the nouns changed: the title says what it is and when, the statement says why it matters, the one beat shows what happens, the outro says what to do.

## On-screen wording

- **One idea per screen.** A caption is one short sentence the eye reads in two seconds.
- **Headlines state the takeaway**, not the topic: *Describe the team in YAML. No code.* rather than *Configuration*.
- **Contrast beats description.** Strike the old idea, state the new one. *Process automation.* → *Collaborative intelligence.*
- **Present tense, active voice, concrete nouns.** *A support human joins from WhatsApp, and answers.*
- **Affirm what it is.** Prefer *The workflow is emergent* to *No central workflow*. When a negation is needed, make it precise: *No predefined workflows*, not *No workflow*.
- **Claim what exists.** *It's a vision, running today* — a vision, and already real. Do not present the product as a promise, and do not undersell it as merely a plan.
- **Name people as participants.** Humans are members of the team, drawn among the others, never off to the side as an audience.
- **Mark who is human and who is AI** with a consistent visual sign and a legend when both appear.
- Short lists are fine in the "under the hood" scenes; elsewhere prefer a sentence.

## The Yuma voice

Editorial, spacious and confident, like the Yuma presentation style: light serif headlines, quiet body text, generous space, restraint with effects.

- Confident without superlatives. No "revolutionary", "seamless", "unlock".
- Warm and plain. Short sentences. Let a pause do the work of an exclamation mark.
- Narration complements the picture; it does not read the caption aloud word for word when a shorter spoken line is clearer.
- Narration is tighter than on-screen text, because speech is slower than reading. If the caption says *One akgent waiting never blocks the rest of the team*, the voice can say *One akgent waiting never blocks the team.*
- End on the brand. For a product made by Yuma, the outro says *Powered by Yuma* — on screen or in the last narration line. When the subject is Yuma itself (an internal event, a team announcement), the Yuma logo is enough; do not say Yuma powers Yuma.

## Product vocabulary

Agree the vocabulary with the user before writing, and apply it everywhere — screen, captions, narration, legend. Decide explicitly where a coined product term replaces the common word and where the common word must stay.

### Akgents

- The product is **Akgents**; the framework and packages are **akgentic**.
- A member of an Akgents team is an **akgent**. Use "akgents" throughout — except in the opening two beats, which address newcomers in the common word: *teams of AI agents that actually work together* and *One agent answers.*
- Package names (`akgentic-agent`, …) are never altered.
- Spoken: "Akgents" is one word with a soft, audible k — not "A-K-Gents", and not a stressed "ACK". Verify by ear with the chosen voice (see `audio.md`). The singular after "an" tends to be spelled out by voices; rephrase with the plural (*one of the akgents*).
- The message: Akgents is **not process automation with one global state**; it is an **actor-model** framework — independent actors, each with its own mailbox and state, exchanging messages. Show it rather than say it:
  - no links drawn in advance — a connection exists only while a message travels;
  - akgents working in parallel; akgents contacting each other directly;
  - an akgent asking a human; a human correcting course mid-task;
  - one human holding several conversations at once;
  - an akgent waiting on another without blocking the team;
  - a support human joining through another channel (chat, messaging);
  - an akgent crashing, and its manager relaunching a fresh one.
- Closing lines that were approved: *No predefined workflows. No global state. Just actors, collaborating.* and *Collaborative intelligence. It's a vision, running today.*
